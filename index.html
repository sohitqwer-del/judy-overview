JUDY explores how a garment photo, a physical reference, and AI-detected landmarks can support measurement.

A research prototype. Human review is required.
Not a system for automatic production grading.

Explore the evidence ↗

The question How much useful measurement can a photo support?

The boundary These results assess labels and pixel geometry, not verified physical accuracy.

01 / Workflow

A reference. A few points.
A person to check them.

The model finds landmarks. Geometry connects them. Human review stays part of the process.

  1. 01

    Set a reference

    A physical reference or markers in the photo provide a basis for scale.

  2. 02

    Find the landmarks

    AI identifies garment points that can anchor measurements.

  3. 03

    Calculate geometry

    Use the landmarks and reference to calculate garment measurements.

  4. 04

    Review the result

    A person checks the points, missing results, and measurement plausibility.

03 / Scope & limitations

A small study.
Read it that way.

Three models were trained for 60 epochs each: 180 epochs in total. An epoch is one pass through the training set.

  • YOLOv8-small 60 epochs
  • YOLO11-nano 60 epochs
  • YOLO26-nano 60 epochs
Training
38 real photos + 76 appearance variants

The variants reuse the original photos. They are not independent samples.

Development
10 photos / 2 garments

A small development set, separate from the historical test described here.

Historical test
12 photos / 3 garments

81 measurements can be calculated from the labels. Repeated use of this set limits claims about performance on new garments.

What this evidence cannot establish

Reliable performance across new garments, verified physical accuracy in millimetres, or readiness for automatic production grading. Known label issues also mean the reported errors need careful interpretation.

04 / Next steps

What needs to be checked next.

YOLO26 remains a candidate. Stronger evidence is needed before changing the default.

  1. 01 /

    Resolve label issues

    Review the annotations so comparisons rest on a more reliable reference.

  2. 02 /

    Test on fresh garments

    Use new, independent examples to check whether the gains hold and to examine large errors.

  3. 03 /

    Check physical measurements

    Compare with verified physical measurements before making claims about millimetre accuracy.

Human review remains required throughout.
